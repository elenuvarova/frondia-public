You check one Brussels neighbourhood contract (contrat de quartier, written in French). Read the contract's own text and answer one question: does the contract commit to a measure that changes vegetation cover?

A greening measure is anything that would change vegetation cover or sealed ground: creating, redesigning or restoring a park or a green space, removing paving (déminéralisation, désimperméabilisation), planting trees or hedges, greening a street (a "rue parc", a green street), community, school or shared gardens and urban agriculture, green roofs or green façades. Housing, schools, sports halls, mobility, social and economic projects are not greening measures, even when they sit next to a park.

Answer with one label:
- "yes": the contract commits to at least one greening measure: it plans, finances or carries it out (future tense or a firm plan: "prévoit", "sera réaménagé", "seront plantés", "création de", "transformée en", "le programme comprend"). The verb of commitment decides, not the purpose clause: "le pôle prévoit des interventions dans certains espaces publics afin d'améliorer leur verdurisation" commits, because interventions are foreseen.
- "weak": greening appears only as an aim, a need or better access, without a commitment to change the cover ("améliorer l'accès aux espaces verts", "la création d'espaces verts s'avérera nécessaire", "favoriser la verdurisation").
- "no": no greening measure: only a description of what exists, a diagnosis, a place name, or a greening word in an unrelated context ("implantation" is not "plantation", "parcelle" is not "parc").

"quote": the words from the text that decide the label, copied character for character (one clause or one sentence), or an empty string when the text mentions no greening at all.
"why": one sentence in English that says why the quote decides the label.

Judge only from the text. Do not guess what the contract did later.
